The damage you sleep through

Grinding happens off-hours, with forces chewing never uses. Here's how to spot it — and stop paying for it in enamel.

The crime scene evidence

Most grinders have no idea. The evidence surfaces indirectly: molar edges worn flat, front teeth that look subtly shorter than old photos, chipped fillings that keep needing repair, morning jaw tightness, dull temple headaches, a partner who's heard the sound. Dentists read these signs at a glance — wear patterns are unambiguous once you know them.

Why sleep grinding is worse than chewing

Chewing is brief, cushioned by food, and reflex-limited. Sleep bruxism is none of those things — sustained clenching and grinding, enamel directly on enamel, repeated nightly. Enamel doesn't grow back; every year of unprotected grinding is subtracted permanently, and the repair bill compounds: worn teeth need restoring, and restorations then face the same forces that wore the teeth.

What a guard changes — and why custom matters

A night guard doesn't stop grinding; it makes grinding cheap. The appliance absorbs the forces so enamel and restorations don't, converting irreversible tooth wear into replaceable appliance wear. Custom matters because worn guards get worn only if they're comfortable: a guard made from records of your actual bite is slim and secure where a boiled drugstore tray is bulky and loose — and appliance work happens to be this practice's home turf.
