How your retainer or night guard is made

From the records taken at your visit to the moment it clicks into place — what happens in between, and why doing it here matters to you.

Step one: a record of your teeth

Every appliance starts with an exact copy of your teeth. Dr. Maskatiya examines you and takes the records the appliance needs — for most retainers and night guards, a detailed impression or scan of the arch the appliance will sit on, plus a record of how your teeth meet.

This is the step that decides whether the finished piece fits. It is done at the chair, by the dentist who will fit the appliance, so anything unusual about your bite is noticed now rather than discovered later.

Step two: designed and made here

At many offices the records are boxed up and shipped to a laboratory, sometimes in another state, where a technician who has never seen you makes the appliance from written instructions. If a question comes up, it travels by phone and email; if the fit is off, the whole thing goes back.

Here, the appliance is made in the practice's own lab, to Dr. Maskatiya's prescription. A question is answered by walking down the hall, and the result is checked against your records before you ever sit down for the fitting.

Step three: the fitting

The dentist who designed the appliance seats it, checks how it sits against your teeth and how your bite closes on it, and adjusts it on the spot. A night guard should feel snug but not tight; a retainer should click into place and stay put without pressure. If something isn't right, it is fixed then and there.

You leave with instructions for wear and cleaning, and one simple rule: if it ever feels different — looser, tighter, a rough edge — call. Small adjustments are quick, and they are done here.

What that means for you

Fewer trips, for a start: no waiting on a shipment before the fitting, and no second round of shipping if a refinement is needed. Faster replacements, too: when a retainer goes missing or a night guard cracks, a new one is made from your records here rather than ordered from somewhere else.

And one accountable person. The dentist who examined you designed the appliance, fitted it, and is the one you see if it ever needs attention. That is the boutique idea, applied to the object in your hand.
