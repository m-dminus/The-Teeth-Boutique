Your child's first visit: a parent's playbook

The first dental visits write a child's lifelong script about the dentist. Here's how to help us write a good one.

Earlier than most parents guess

The widely used guidance: first visit around the first birthday, or within months of the first tooth. That sounds early until you know what it's for — not heroic dentistry, but familiarity, a lap-exam of the new teeth, catching early bottle-related decay, and answering your questions about teething, thumbs, and toothpaste while the stakes are tiny.

How to prepare without teaching fear

Children borrow their scripts from adults, so the preparation is mostly vocabulary discipline. Keep it boring and positive: 'the dentist counts your teeth and makes sure they're strong.' Avoid the accidental fear curriculum — no 'it won't hurt' (which introduces the idea), no 'be brave' (ditto), and no loaning them your own dental history. Books and pretend-play about dentist visits do more good than pep talks.

What a good first visit looks like

Short, unhurried, and paced entirely to the child: instruments introduced as toys-with-jobs before they're used, a ride in the chair, teeth counted, maybe a gentle polish if the day allows — and a child who leaves with a sticker and no dramatic memories. That's a complete success, even if less 'dentistry' happened than a parent expected. Trust built now is what makes every future visit uneventful.

At The Teeth Boutique, all ages share one practice — under-18s accompanied by a parent or guardian — and Dr. Maskatiya's calm, warm manner does its best work on exactly these visits.
