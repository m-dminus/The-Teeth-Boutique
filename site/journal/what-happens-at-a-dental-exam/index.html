What actually happens at a dental exam

Half of dental anxiety is not knowing what's going on. So here's the whole exam, demystified — step by step.

More than looking for cavities

A thorough exam is a systematic survey, and cavities are only one line of it. Teeth are checked for decay, cracks, and wear; every existing filling and crown is inspected for aging margins; gums are assessed — often with gentle measurements — for the pocketing that signals periodontal disease; your bite is evaluated; and the soft tissues of the mouth are screened for abnormalities, which is the quiet oral-cancer check most patients never notice happening.

Why X-rays, and how often

Much of dentistry's territory is invisible: between teeth, under old fillings, below the gumline, inside bone. Digital X-rays cover that ground — with modern digital imaging cutting exposure time roughly in half compared with traditional film — and they're taken on a schedule matched to your risk, not reflexively at every visit.

At this practice, imaging is shown, not just taken: findings go up on a screen, including intraoral camera views of the actual tooth in question, so you're looking at the evidence alongside the dentist.

The conversation that should follow

A good exam ends in candor: what's healthy, what's worth watching, what needs treatment and how soon, and what each path costs. Watching is a legitimate clinical plan — not everything found needs drilling — and a dentist who sometimes says 'let's recheck it in six months' is showing you the same judgment they'll use when treatment really is warranted.
