Dentures

Care for the appliance, and the mouth under it

A denture serves for years when two things stay healthy: the appliance itself, and the gums and bone it rests on. Both need attention.

Daily care is straightforward but non-negotiable: clean the denture daily with a denture brush and cleanser (not abrasive toothpaste), keep it moist when it's out, and give your gums a rest period — typically overnight. Clean your gums, tongue, and any remaining teeth as well; the mouth under the appliance matters as much as the appliance.

Just as important are denture exams. Ridges change shape over time, fits loosen gradually, and sore spots or slippage are signals — not normal aging to be endured. Regular examination lets us adjust or reline before a small problem becomes a remake, and lets Dr. Maskatiya check the health of the tissues underneath, including screening the soft tissues of the mouth.

Good to know

Questions, answered

My denture feels loose. Do I just use more adhesive?

No — escalating adhesive is a signal, not a solution. A loosening fit usually means your ridges have changed and the denture needs a reline. It's a maintenance procedure, not a failure.

How often should denture wearers be examined?

Regularly, even with no natural teeth remaining — to check fit, adjust wear points, and examine the health of gums and soft tissue under and around the appliance. We'll recommend an interval for your situation.
