Dentures · Implants

A denture that holds still

Implants anchor the appliance, so it snaps into place instead of floating on the gums — the difference patients describe as getting their confidence back.

The chronic complaint with conventional dentures — especially lowers — is movement: shifting while eating, caution while laughing, adhesive as a daily ritual. An implant-retained denture answers this mechanically. A small number of implants are placed in the jaw, and the denture attaches to them, gaining a stability gums alone can't provide.

The result sits between a conventional denture and a full arch of implants in both cost and experience: dramatically more secure than a traditional denture, more economical than replacing every tooth individually. For many long-time denture wearers, it's the upgrade that changes how they eat and speak in company.

Candidacy depends on bone and health factors that only an examination can establish — the consultation gives you a straight answer either way.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Is my existing denture wasted?

Not necessarily — depending on its condition and design, an existing denture can sometimes be adapted to attachments. Your exam will settle it either way.

Do I still take it out at night?

Implant-retained dentures are typically removable for cleaning and overnight rest — you get stability by day and easy hygiene by night.
