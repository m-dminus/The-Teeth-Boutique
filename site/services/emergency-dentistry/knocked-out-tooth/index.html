Emergency · Minutes matter

The hour that saves a tooth

A knocked-out permanent tooth is the one dental emergency where speed changes everything. Here's the drill — then call 773-857-2290.

Call now: 773-857-2290

Do this now

Five moves, in order

  1. Pick it up by the crown

    The chewing end — never the root. The root's surface cells are what make reattachment possible, and fingers destroy them.

  2. Rinse briefly if dirty

    A few seconds under cool water. No scrubbing, no soap, no wrapping in tissue — that kills the cells you're trying to save.

  3. Try to reseat it

    For an adult tooth: gently place it back in its socket facing the right way and hold it with light bite pressure on gauze or cloth. This is the best transport there is.

  4. Can't reseat? Keep it moist

    Cold milk is the classic carrier; saline works; held inside your cheek if you're an adult and alert. Never dry, never in plain water for long.

  5. Get here within the hour

    Call 773-857-2290 on the way. Reimplantation succeeds best in the first 30–60 minutes.

One critical exception: baby teeth are not reimplanted — forcing one back can damage the adult tooth developing underneath. If a child knocks out a tooth and you're unsure which kind it is, keep the tooth moist, call us, and we'll walk you through it.

Even when a tooth can't be saved, the socket needs prompt attention, and modern replacement — from bridges to implants — means a lost tooth is a solvable problem. But the save is always the better story, so move fast.
