Cosmetic

Small flaws, gone by dinner

Bonding sculpts shade-matched composite directly onto the tooth — chips, gaps, and uneven edges corrected in a single sitting.

Dental bonding is cosmetic dentistry at its most immediate: tooth-colored composite resin applied to the tooth, sculpted by hand, hardened with light, and polished until it vanishes into the enamel around it. A chipped corner rebuilt, a small gap closed, a short tooth lengthened, a discolored spot masked — frequently in one appointment, usually without anesthetic.

Its virtues are speed, economy, and conservatism — most bonding removes little or no tooth structure, making it one of the few cosmetic treatments that's essentially reversible. Its real limits: composite can stain over years and lacks porcelain's longevity, so for larger transformations veneers remain the premium instrument. We'll tell you which side of that line your case sits on.

Good to know

Questions, answered

How long does bonding last?

Commonly years, varying with location, bite forces, and habits — biting nails or pens is bonding's natural enemy. Repairs are straightforward when the day comes.

Will it match my tooth?

Yes — shade is selected against your neighboring enamel and the finish polished to match its gloss. Done well, you'll lose track of which tooth it was.
