Cosmetic

A new surface, your smile

Veneers are thin ceramic facings bonded to the front of teeth — the instrument for changing shade, shape, and proportion all at once.

A veneer wraps the visible face of a tooth in fine porcelain — masking deep discoloration whitening can't touch, rebuilding worn or chipped edges, closing small gaps, and bringing uneven teeth into a harmonious line. Porcelain earns its reputation here: it carries the same translucency and light-play as natural enamel, and it resists staining better than the real thing.

The craft is design. Good veneer work is planned around your face — lip line, symmetry, the proportions that photograph as 'great smile' rather than 'great dental work.' We plan cases tooth by tooth with you seeing the plan before it's executed, and with shade decisions made in true light against your natural teeth.

Veneers are a commitment: preparing a tooth for porcelain is not reversible, and that's said in so many words at the consultation, along with what your case actually requires — sometimes fewer veneers than you expected, sometimes whitening and bonding instead.

What to expect

The veneer process

  1. Design consultation

    What you want changed, what your teeth allow, and the plan — agreed before anything begins.

  2. Preparation & records

    A conservative layer of enamel is prepared, records captured, and temporaries placed so you preview the direction.

  3. The fitting

    Each veneer is tried, checked against light and lip line, and bonded only when both of us are satisfied.

  4. The long run

    Veneers live like teeth: brush, floss, exams — plus a night guard if you grind, to protect the porcelain.

Good to know

Questions, answered

How many veneers will I need?

Only as many as the goal requires — sometimes one or two artfully matched, sometimes a full smile line. Beware anyone whose answer is always the bigger number; ours comes from your lip line and your goals.

How long do veneers last?

Well-made, well-bonded porcelain commonly serves for many years. The threats are grinding and gumline decay — a night guard handles the first, ordinary excellent hygiene the second.
