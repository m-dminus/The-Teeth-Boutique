Cosmetic

Brighter, believably

Professional whitening lifts the years of coffee, tea, and red wine out of enamel — supervised, calibrated, and aimed at 'naturally lucky,' not 'blue-white.'

Over-the-counter strips have their place, but professional whitening is a different instrument: stronger agents, applied with your gums properly protected, supervised by people who know what your enamel can handle — including systems like Zoom for patients who want the change delivered in-office. The result arrives faster, goes further, and is calibrated to a shade that flatters your face rather than glowing in photographs.

The honest print: whitening works on natural enamel, not on crowns, veneers, or bonding — which is why whitening comes first and restorations are matched after. Some sensitivity during treatment is common and passes; deep internal discoloration sometimes calls for a different tool entirely, like bonding or veneers, and we'll say so at the exam rather than sell you the wrong fix.

Results last longest with unheroic maintenance: rinse after the espresso, keep your cleanings, expect an occasional touch-up. Staining is chemistry, and chemistry keeps trying.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Will whitening damage my enamel?

Professionally supervised whitening is considered safe for enamel — the common side effect is temporary sensitivity, which is managed and passes. Supervision is precisely what the professional version buys you.

How white will my teeth get?

It varies by your starting shade and the nature of the staining — surface stains respond dramatically, deep internal discoloration less so. You'll get a grounded prediction at your exam, before you spend anything.
