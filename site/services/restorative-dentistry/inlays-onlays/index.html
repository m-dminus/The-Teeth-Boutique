Restorative

Between filling and crown

Some damage outgrows a filling but doesn't justify grinding a tooth down for a crown. Inlays and onlays occupy that middle ground beautifully.

An inlay is a custom-fabricated restoration that fits within the contours of a tooth's chewing surface; an onlay extends further, capping one or more cusps. Both are made outside the mouth from durable, shade-matched material and then bonded precisely into place — bringing lab-made strength and fit to repairs where a large direct filling would be structurally weak.

The philosophy is conservation: a crown requires reshaping the entire tooth, while an inlay or onlay preserves every millimeter of healthy structure that doesn't need to go. For the right tooth — typically a molar with substantial decay or an aging large filling, but sound remaining walls — it's the proportionate answer.

Which restoration your tooth actually needs is a judgment call made from examination and imaging, and it's one we'll walk you through with the pictures on screen.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Inlay, onlay, or crown — how is it decided?

By how much sound tooth remains and where the forces run. Enough healthy structure: inlay or onlay preserves it. Compromised walls or cracks: a crown protects better. You'll see the reasoning, not just the recommendation.

How many visits?

Typically the tooth is prepared and recorded at one visit and the finished restoration bonded at a subsequent one. We'll map your specific sequence when treatment is planned.
