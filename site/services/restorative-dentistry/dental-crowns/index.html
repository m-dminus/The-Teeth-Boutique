Restorative

Full armor for a hard-working tooth

When a tooth has lost too much structure to stand on its own, a crown wraps it in strength — and done well, in invisibility.

A crown is a custom cap covering the entire visible tooth, restoring shape, strength, and appearance in one piece. It's the standard of care for teeth cracked or broken beyond a filling's help, hollowed by large old restorations, treated with root canal therapy, or worn down by years of grinding — and it's how a single dark or misshapen tooth can be brought back into line with its neighbors.

The craft is in the details: a preparation that removes no more structure than necessary, records that capture your tooth and bite precisely, a shade chosen against your natural teeth in good light, and a fit checked at the margins where crowns succeed or fail. You're numbed for preparation, protected with a temporary between visits, and the final crown is adjusted until your bite closes without a thought.

A crown's enemies are the same as a tooth's: decay at the margin and grinding forces from above. Regular exams watch the first; if you grind, a night guard defends against the second.

Good to know

Questions, answered

How long does a crown last?

Commonly many years — often a decade or more — with hygiene and bite forces the deciding factors. The crown can't decay, but the tooth at its margin can, which is what exams monitor.

Will it look fake?

Not done properly. Shade, translucency, and shape are matched to your neighboring teeth — the goal is a crown your own eye stops finding.
