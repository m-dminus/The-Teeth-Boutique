Restorative

Repairs that disappear

A filling ends decay and rebuilds the tooth — and with shade-matched composite, does it invisibly.

A cavity is decay that has breached enamel; a filling removes the decayed portion and rebuilds the tooth's shape and function. Modern tooth-colored composite bonds directly to tooth structure, which lets the preparation stay conservative — removing what's diseased and little else — and lets the finished repair match the tooth so closely it shouldn't announce itself.

Placement is methodical: the tooth is numbed and isolated, decay is removed with precision instruments, and the composite is layered, shaped to your bite, hardened, and polished. Most fillings are complete in a single visit, and mild post-treatment sensitivity, when it occurs, typically settles within days.

We'll also discuss materials openly when your case calls for it — composite is the aesthetic default, and the right choice for a given tooth depends on its location, the size of the repair, and the forces it will carry.

Good to know

Questions, answered

How do I know if I have a cavity?

Often you don't — early decay is usually painless, which is what exams and X-rays are for. Sensitivity to sweets or cold, food catching, or a visible dark spot are later signals worth a prompt look.

How long do fillings last?

Years — with lifespan driven by size, location, bite forces, and hygiene. Fillings are checked at every exam and replaced when margins wear, ideally before anything breaks.
