General & Preventive

The disease that whispers

Gum disease rarely hurts until it's advanced — it bleeds a little, recedes a little, and quietly loosens the foundations. It's also very treatable, especially early.

Periodontal disease is an infection of the structures holding your teeth: gums, ligament, and bone. It begins as gingivitis — red, puffy gums that bleed with brushing — which is fully reversible with professional cleaning and improved home care. Left alone, it can progress to periodontitis, where gums detach from teeth, pockets form and deepen, and the bone itself erodes. Among adults, this process — not decay — is the leading cause of tooth loss.

The warning signs are easy to dismiss: bleeding when you brush or floss, persistent bad breath, gums pulling away from teeth, new sensitivity at the gumline, teeth that feel subtly looser. None of these is 'normal.' All of them are reasons for an evaluation, where gum measurements and X-rays establish exactly where you stand.

Treatment is staged to severity: professional cleaning and hygiene coaching for gingivitis; deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) when pockets have formed; locally applied antibiotic therapy such as Arestin where deep pockets need extra help; and a periodontal maintenance rhythm to hold ground once it's regained.

What to expect

The treatment ladder

  1. Evaluate & measure

    Pocket depths charted, X-rays reviewed — you'll see the map of where disease is and isn't.

  2. Treat to severity

    From standard cleaning to scaling and root planing, with Arestin placed in stubborn pockets where indicated.

  3. Re-measure and prove it

    Healing is verified with numbers, not vibes — pockets are re-charted to confirm the response.

  4. Maintain the win

    Periodontal maintenance visits on a tighter interval keep bacteria from re-establishing below the gumline.

Good to know

Questions, answered

My gums bleed when I floss. Is that normal?

Common, yes. Normal, no. Bleeding gums are inflamed gums — usually early, reversible gingivitis. Keep flossing gently and book an evaluation; this is the cheap stage to fix.

Can gum disease affect the rest of my health?

Periodontal disease has been associated with several systemic conditions in research, and it's a chronic infection either way — reason enough to treat it seriously. Your medical history is part of how we plan your periodontal care.
