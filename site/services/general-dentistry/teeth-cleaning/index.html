General & Preventive

What your toothbrush can't do

Once plaque hardens into tartar, no home tool removes it. Professional cleaning exists because chemistry doesn't negotiate.

Even excellent brushers miss spots, and any plaque that lingers mineralizes into calculus — tartar — which bonds to the tooth and shelters the bacteria that inflame gums. Professional cleaning removes that buildup above and just below the gumline with instruments designed for exactly this, then polishes the tooth surfaces smooth, lifting everyday surface stains along the way.

The payoff is bigger than fresh-mouth feel. Regular cleanings are the front line against gingivitis and its escalation into periodontal disease — the quiet condition that, left alone, costs adults more teeth than decay does. Twice a year is the standard rhythm for healthy mouths; your interval may differ, and we'll tell you why if so.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Does a cleaning hurt?

A routine cleaning on healthy gums is comfortable — expect scraping sensations and some pressure. Tender, inflamed gums may feel more; that tenderness is itself information, and it improves as your gums get healthier.

Will cleaning whiten my teeth?

It removes surface stains — coffee, tea, tobacco — restoring your natural shade. Changing the underlying shade is whitening's job, and we offer that separately.

What if it's been years?

Then let's look before we plan: heavy buildup or gum pocketing may call for a deep cleaning rather than a standard one. No judgment either way — just the right procedure for the state of things.
