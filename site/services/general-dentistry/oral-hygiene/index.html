General & Preventive

The 363 days we don't see you

Dentistry happens a few days a year. Your habits run every day in between — which makes them the most powerful instrument in this building.

The fundamentals are unglamorous and undefeated: brush twice daily for two full minutes with fluoride toothpaste, angle the bristles toward the gumline where plaque actually collects, and clean between your teeth daily — floss or interdental brushes — because a toothbrush physically cannot reach nearly a third of each tooth's surface.

What we add is personalization. At your hygiene visits we'll tell you — specifically and kindly — where your technique is missing: the molar corner you consistently skip, the gumline where inflammation says the brush isn't arriving, whether an electric brush or a water flosser would actually help your particular hands and mouth or just cost you money.

Diet gets the same plain treatment: frequency of sugar matters more than quantity, sipping sweet or acidic drinks across hours is harder on enamel than finishing them, and water after anything is the cheapest oral health habit that exists.

Good to know

Questions, answered

Electric or manual toothbrush?

A well-used manual beats a poorly-used electric — but most people aren't well-used manuals. Electric brushes with timers fix the two commonest failures: too little time, too much force. We'll tell you if you'd actually benefit.

Is flossing really necessary?

Something must clean between your teeth daily — floss, picks, or interdental brushes all count. The spaces between teeth are where a toothbrush can't go and where a great deal of decay and gum disease begins.
