Facial Aesthetics

Restore, don't reinvent

Fillers replace volume where time has thinned it — smoothing folds and restoring contour with results you can see immediately.

Where Botox addresses lines written by movement, dermal fillers address what's present at rest: folds around the nose and mouth, thinning lips, hollows where fullness used to live. A smooth gel — commonly based on hyaluronic acid, a substance your skin produces naturally — is placed precisely where structure has diminished.

Results are visible immediately and settle over the following days, typically lasting months and varying by product and placement. In a face that smiles, speaks, and chews, placement judgment matters enormously — which is where a clinician's working knowledge of facial anatomy pays off.

As with everything aesthetic in this practice, the consultation is candid: what filler would truly improve, what it wouldn't, and what amount stops at 'refreshed' rather than 'noticeable.'

Good to know

Questions, answered

Botox or filler — which do I need?

Different tools for different problems: Botox for lines that appear with expression, filler for folds and volume loss present at rest. Many patients benefit from one, some from both — the consultation sorts your case cleanly.

Does it hurt?

Expect brief pinches and pressure; many filler products include a numbing agent, and topical anesthetic can be used. Mild swelling or tenderness for a day or two afterward is common.
